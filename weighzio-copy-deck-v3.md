## 0. The product, as it is being sold

- **Modules are sold individually. There is no mandatory core.** Every module stands alone.
- Digital waste tracking functionality sits **inside the Skip Hire module**.
- **Custom modules** can be built for requirements outside the standard range.
- **Discount increases with the number of modules taken.**

- Call to action is **enquire / find out more**, never buy now.
- Some modules can feed job and enquiry data into Google Ads and Meta campaigns.

**Trade mark:** `weighzIO®`. Use the ® on first prominent use on each page (hero, footer) and plain `weighzIO` thereafter. Note the capitalisation: lowercase w, capital I and O.

**Voice:** plain and direct. Yard and transport managers, not IT directors. No "streamline", "seamless", "revolutionise". No exclamation marks. Short sentences.

## 4. Digital Waste Tracking guide

**This is the highest-value page on the site.** "Digital waste tracking" does the same volume as the category head term and is up 900% year on year. Build it as a cluster hub, not a single article.

**Deliberately out of scope.** "Waste transfer note" and "waste carrier licence" each do 5,000 a month, but that traffic is waste producers, van operators and small traders looking for free templates and licence application help. They will not buy operations software, and chasing those terms dilutes the page's topical focus. Do not write template downloads, licence application guides, or "how much does a waste carriers licence cost" content.

**H1:** Digital Waste Tracking Explained: What UK Waste Operators Must Do

**Structure.** Each numbered heading below is an H2, and several are written to carry a secondary search term.

1. **The short version.** Four bullets: dates, who's in scope, what has to be recorded, what it replaces.
2. **Does this apply to me?** Do you hold a permit or licence to receive waste? Yes, in scope since 1 October 2026. No, but you carry waste? October 2027, with receiving sites asking for better data well before that. Say plainly that their permit is the document that settles it and that neither a software company nor a web page is the right place to decide their legal position.
3. **The dates in full.** England and Wales 1 October 2026. Scotland and Northern Ireland January 2027. Carriers, brokers and dealers October 2027. Exemptions and green list waste in later phases.
4. **What has to be recorded for every load.** EWC code, SIC code, movement reference, origin, destination, quantity, treatment outcome, persistent organic pollutants flag on every load. This is the section people bookmark.
5. **Digital waste transfer notes: what changes.** Targets "digital waste transfer note", 500/mo and up 900%. Cover how the digital record relates to the paper note, and what operators do differently day to day.
6. **Waste duty of care under the new regime.** Targets "waste duty of care", 500/mo. What the duty is, what evidences it now, and what operators are still required to keep.
7. **Hazardous waste consignment notes.** Targets "hazardous waste consignment note", 500/mo. How consignments are handled and reported.
8. **The two working day rule**, and that the load isn't recorded until the system issues a digital waste record number.
9. **The two submission routes.** API through your software, or Defra's spreadsheet upload, which is temporary. Be fair about the spreadsheet.
10. **What it costs.** £26 a year per legal entity that creates or edits records.
11. **What to do before your deadline.** Five practical steps, no product mention.
12. **Official sources.** Link to the GOV.UK digital waste tracking policy paper and the legislation.
13. **One soft CTA at the foot.** "weighzIO files these records automatically as part of the Skip Hire module. See how."

**Verify before publishing:** exactly how much of the paper duty of care regime falls away in phase 1. Sources disagree and sections 5 and 6 both depend on getting it right.

## 6. Claude Design brief

### Design system

**Built and verified in Claude Design, 9 September 2026.** Tokens, components and colour rules are in the system, so screens inherit them. Verified against the exported CSS:

- CTA states all pass WCAG AA with a navy label: base `#F47D2D` 5.20:1, hover `#F79C5E` 6.57:1, press `#EA782B` 4.80:1.
- CTA glow is blur-only, zero offset, zero spread: `0 0 18px 0 rgba(244,125,45,.45)` pulsing to `0 0 32px 0 rgba(244,125,45,.30)` over 2.8s. Border `#F9B183`, lighter than the fill. Hover lightens the border and scales to 1.025 with the pulse still running. Press darkens, scales to 0.985, inset shadow, 70ms down and 200ms spring back. No `translateY` anywhere.
- Link hover is navy with an underline, not orange.
- Cards and module tiles carry `--shadow-sm` at rest, stepping to `--shadow-md` on hover, hover scale 1.01.
- `--shadow-on-ink` and `.wz-card--on-ink` exist for panels on the navy bands.
- `.wz-module--custom` is white fill with an orange border, never a solid orange block.
- Type is Outfit for display and IBM Plex Sans for body and data, chosen because it ships real tabular figures. Both are substitutions: no font binaries were supplied. Swap if licensed families exist.

**One outstanding fix.** `.wz-module--custom .wz-module__title` uses `--orange-500` `#DC6A1E`, which measures 3.43:1 on white and fails AA for text. Change it to navy `--navy-700` and let the orange border do the distinguishing, or darken the orange to at least 4.5:1. Can be fixed in the CSS at build time rather than in Claude Design.

### Visual direction

**Light base, navy as punctuation.** Closer to the Pearce Group landing pages than the conservatory site. This is bought by a transport manager comparing options, so the page has to be read and scanned, not felt.

- **Navy hero** with the reverse logo, then a light body, then two full-bleed navy bands to break up the length: the digital waste tracking section (2.4) and the final CTA (2.10). Keeps the brand present without committing the site to dark.
- **Light background is not a style preference, it's a constraint.** Sixteen navy module cards on a dark background turn to mush and lose the equal-weight "pick yours" argument. The five UI mock-ups have white working areas and would float badly on dark. The guide page is long-form regulatory reading that people will print.
- **Typography needs tabular numerals.** There's a discount table, plus weights, tonnages and percentage splits across the mock-ups. Figures that don't align vertically undermine software credibility instantly. Geometric sans for headings to echo the logo, screen-native grotesque for body and data.
- **No stock photography.** No lorries, no skips, no hard hats. The UI screens are the imagery. One authentic yard photo if a real one exists, otherwise none. Stock waste imagery reads as a broker, not a software company.
- **Burst device** from the logo as the section divider and the tick marker throughout.

### Colour rules

Two colours only. No green, no third hue. The risk with a two-colour palette is that orange gets used for everything and stops meaning anything, so it is rationed:

| Use | Colour |
|---|---|
| Primary CTA, filled | Orange `#F47D2D`, navy label |
| Burst device, as divider and tick marker | Orange, small |
| Everything else: headings, body, eyebrow labels, module card fills | Navy `#122D4B` |
| Custom Modules card | Navy fill, orange border and orange heading |

**Nothing except the CTA gets a solid orange fill.** The Custom Modules card stands out through its border and heading, not by becoming a second orange block competing with the buttons.

**Green is reserved for one thing only:** the "Filed to DWT" status state inside the mock-ups. It is a semantic status colour meaning compliant. If green also decorates bullets across the site it stops reading as a status, and the filed state is the single most important detail in the whole mock-up set.

**Inside the UI mock-ups**, orange stays for the primary action and the filed state as specced. A screenshot is a separate visual context, so it doesn't compete with the page's own buttons.

**If the light sections feel flat once designed**, the fix is a pale orange tint on alternating section backgrounds, `#F47D2D` at 4 to 6% opacity. Warmth without introducing a colour and without weakening the CTA. Do that before reaching for a third hue.

**Contrast, measured:** navy on orange is 5.20:1 and passes AA. White on orange is 2.69:1 and fails. Button labels are navy.

Navy `#122D4B` chrome, orange `#F47D2D` for primary actions, green for the filed state only, white working area, realistic Midlands place names, real EWC codes, sensible tonnages. 2. **Load composition.** Waste types as a percentage split totalling 100, bulky items listed separately, three contamination photo thumbnails, variance between requested and recorded weight.

## 8. Build notes

### 8.0 Getting the design out of Claude Design and into Claude Code

**Do this before there is anything to transfer.** The Pearce Group build lost time here twice: once to the wrong export format, once to an auth wall hit mid-build.

**Step 1. Authorise first, in an interactive session.** Open VS Code, open a Claude Code session in the terminal, and run:

```
claude mcp add --scope user --transport http claude-design https://api.anthropic.com/v1/design/mcp
/design-login
```

Complete the browser sign-in. `/design-login` cannot finish its OAuth flow in a non-interactive session, which is what blocked Pearce. Once done, it carries over.

**Step 2. Transfer with Send to, never Export.** In Claude Design use **Send to → Local coding agent**. Do **not** use "Export as standalone HTML".

That export gives a `.dc.html` canvas bundle, not a web page: an outer runtime harness (`trustedOrigin`, `mountPage`, `pendingFrames`, `postMessage` iframe plumbing) wrapping a `__bundler/manifest` script of base64-encoded assets, with the real page stored as an escaped JSON string. On Pearce that was 5.4MB and needed stripping back to 72KB by hand.

**Step 3. Tell Claude Code the target shape before it writes anything.** Paste this with the handoff:

> Target is a single `index.html` with a client-side router, plain CSS in a `<style>` block, no build step and no utility-class framework. Images as separate files in `/images` referenced by relative path, never base64. Fonts via one Google Fonts link scoped to the weights actually used, not self-hosted `@font-face`. Semantic headings throughout: one H1 per route, real H2s for sections. Strip any Claude Design canvas runtime and any unused design-system variables or classes that came across.

**Step 4. Check before building on top.** Open the file and confirm: size is tens of KB not megabytes, no `mountPage` or `postMessage` code, images are real files, headings are real headings. Fixing this now is minutes. Fixing it after three rounds of styling is hours.

**Fallback if the handoff still fails.** Export the HTML, then give Claude Code the file and the same instruction as step 3, adding: decode the base64 assets out to `/images` with descriptive filenames and replace every `src="<uuid>"` and `url("<uuid>")` reference with the relative path.

### 8.0a Hooking it up to GitHub and Vercel

Run from inside the project folder, once the file exists:

```
git init
git add -A
git commit -m "Initial weighzIO site"
gh repo create weighzio-site --private --source=. --push
```

If `gh` isn't installed, create the empty repo on github.com instead, then:

```
git remote add origin https://github.com/<your-account>/weighzio-site.git
git branch -M main
git push -u origin main
```

**Check the account before pushing.** Pearce pushed under the wrong GitHub account first. `git remote -v` confirms where it's actually going.

Then in Vercel: New Project, import the repo, framework preset **Other**, no build command, output directory root. It deploys on every push to `main` and gives you the `.vercel.app` URL to review on. Point the real domain at it later.

Ongoing, after any change:

```
git add -A && git commit -m "message" && git push
```

### 8.1 Stack and conventions

**Single `index.html` with a client-side router, same pattern as the conservatory site.** Files on the laptop, edited by Claude Code in VS Code, committed and pushed to a GitHub repo, Vercel deploys from that repo. Built and reviewed on the `.vercel.app` URL, real domain pointed at Vercel once it exists.

- `vercel.json` with a catch-all rewrite sending every path to `/index.html`, excluding `/api/*`. That serves `/modules`, `/digital-waste-tracking`, `/enquire` and `/privacy`.
- Router updates `document.title`, the meta description **and the canonical tag** on every route change. The conservatory site does the first two; canonical matters more here.
- Generate canonical and OG URLs from `window.location.origin` rather than hardcoding them, so nothing needs editing when the domain is pointed.
- Form posts to `/api/enquire.js` in the repo root. Vercel picks it up with no config. See section 5.
- Ask the designer for an SVG logo. Trimmed transparent, reverse and favicon PNGs are already handed over.
- Module enquiry links pass the module name as a query parameter and pre-tick the checkbox.
- Cookie banner declines non-essential by default.
- Mobile first.
- `#F47D2D` on white fails contrast on small text. Navy for body copy, orange for large text, buttons and the burst device only.
- Test the guide page in print view.

## 8a. Launch day checklist

Work down it in order. Nothing here takes more than ten minutes.

**Before pointing the domain**

- [ ] `robots.txt` on the `.vercel.app` URL disallows everything, so the staging URL never gets indexed
- [ ] Every route tested: `/`, `/modules`, `/digital-waste-tracking`, `/enquire`, `/privacy`
- [ ] Hard refresh on each route, not just clicking through the nav, to confirm the rewrite works on direct entry
- [ ] Title, meta description and canonical each change correctly per route
- [ ] Form submits and the email arrives, including from mobile
- [ ] Module enquiry links pre-tick the right checkbox
- [ ] Checked on iOS Safari and Android Chrome, not only desktop

**Pointing the domain**

- [ ] All four domains added in Vercel, `weighz.io` set as primary, the other three set to 301 redirect
- [ ] DNS records updated at the registrar
- [ ] HTTPS certificate issued and confirmed working on the canonical
- [ ] One canonical version only: pick `www` or non-`www` and redirect the other
- [ ] `robots.txt` updated to allow crawling
- [ ] SPF and DKIM records added for the sending domain
- [ ] Form endpoint swapped from Web3Forms to own-domain sending, and retested
